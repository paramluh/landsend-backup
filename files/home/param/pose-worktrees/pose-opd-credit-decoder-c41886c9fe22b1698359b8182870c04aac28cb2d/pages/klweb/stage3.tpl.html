Cross-channel dependence: how much each channel relies on the other

How much worse each channel predicts its own words when the other speaker's channel is taken away: the rise in normalised word CE over the real conversation, on 32 held-out stage-3 conversations with both speaker placements and both channels' words pooled. Higher = more reliance on the other speaker; 0 = ignores them. Orange = other speaker silent: their text track has no words at all and their audio is the codec's real silence. Green = other speaker absent: their channel is left out of the input entirely. Blue (diamond points) = other speaker's whole track swapped for another conversation's: both their text track and their audio come from a different held-out conversation (same speaker role), so the partner is plausible but wrong; branches draw this as long dashes with diamonds. If the model really listens to the other speaker, this should hurt more than silent / absent. Short dashes = ★ stage-3 checkpoints; long dashes with dots = Demeter (Hestia) checkpoints through the identical test (same conversations, same silence codes, our word CE) in Demeter's own input format. InteractionBench: how good the back-and-forth is, per checkpoint

Each checkpoint continues 24 fixed held-out conversations (20 s of the real conversation, then 120 s where the model generates both speakers; 4 samples each; the training system prompt) and is scored 0-100 against the real continuations of the same conversations. Turn dynamics (40): how real the timing is (switches per minute, response gaps, overlaps, backchannels and where they fall, interruptions, monologue length, floor balance, shared silence). Responsiveness (40): a Qwen3-32B judge reads each turn change and asks whether the new turn answers or builds on what was just said, whether questions get answered, and whether the topic jumps. Degeneration / dead air (20): both silent for more than 2 s, a muted speaker, loops, filler runs, text vs audio agreement. Top: total score (★ thick line, one dot per checkpoint; whiskers in the tooltip = 90% bootstrap interval) and the three parts ×100 (thin). Green line = the real conversations themselves (the ceiling); grey dashed = real speakers paired with the wrong partner (B from another episode), the score of no interaction at all; grey dotted = the right partner 150 s out of sync. Checkpoints before step 3021 were trained without persona text, so they get the voice-clip-only system prompt they were trained with. Bottom: three key numbers as a ratio to the real conversations (1.0 = real): speaker switches per minute, median response gap, and the share of turn changes the judge calls responsive.
