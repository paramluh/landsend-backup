What counts as a jump

The source pathology is a missing pose during dark frames: the last pose was held until the video returned, then jumped immediately to a later pose. The checker targets the resulting temporal discontinuity; it does not require a frozen pose before the jump. It requires a large change across one frame boundary and a medium/high-confidence destination (yellow/green, at least 0.4). Hand entry alone does not count. The previous checker admitted ordinary gestures; its v4 shortlist is also superseded. The revised v5 checker runs over the entire saved frame population, with explicit measurements and rejection reasons. The original jump and synthetic held-pose cuts are positive controls; continuous gestures and the user-marked false positive are negative controls. Rollout-000243 was reclassified as a false positive; the current checker already rejects it. Passing these controls does not establish visual precision or recall.

Every rollout contains 5.28 seconds of saved prefill and 54.72 seconds of generated motion (the original UI prefill remains 5.12 seconds). Sampling uses resident models and fixed 64-row CUDA graphs on 12 two-GPU teams. No one-minute avatar videos are rendered for scanning.

Training-mask audit

The exact exported bandfix355 source, config and job33072 log confirm that pose-loss masking was active. The declared mask also covers the first three pose-token frames after a dark interval, so an unmasked immediate return target is not supported as the explanation. Held poses and the transition remain in the model's causal input history; speech/text losses and later valid pose losses remain active. That is a possible influence, not an established cause. Actual training mask arrays and matching index/video rows were not shipped, so coverage of particular intervals remains unverified.

The original v1 discontinuity

The original reference used pose CFG; all audit rollouts use CFG off as requested. Source frames 511 → 512, 20.44 → 20.48 seconds, corresponding to 36 → 37 seconds in the slowed inspection clip. Median face displacement is 7.96 px and shoulder displacement is 3.03 px. Decoded hand confidence changes sharply: left 0.374 → 0.686, right 0.154 → 0.530; face confidence stays high. These are values in the decoded pose's confidence channel.

Removing tail padding, truncating future tokens and shifting the event away from decoder frame 512 leave its coordinates identical. The discontinuity is present in the generated pose sequence, rather than being introduced by the overlay or that decoder boundary. This does not establish the underlying model cause.
